# Tier 1 Two-Minute Ripple Check Example

This is a tiny non-expert example for learning the MathGov framework and RippleLogic rhythm. It is not a full RLS calculation, validator, legal instrument, or high-stakes decision record.

## Scenario

A parent is deciding whether to answer work messages during dinner with their children.

## Step 1 - Ground reality

Relevant facts: dinner is 30 minutes; the message is not an emergency; the children have been asking for attention; the parent can answer later. Unknowns: whether the sender expects an immediate reply.

Action from Reality Grounding: narrow the claim. This is not a crisis-response decision; it is an attention and family-rhythm decision.

## Step 2 - Protect rights and basic dignity

No formal rights violation is present, but the children have a legitimate relational interest in presence, care, and predictable attention. The parent also has a work responsibility, but not an immediate emergency duty.

Action from Rights Floor: no hard veto, but avoid treating family attention as infinitely tradeable.

## Step 3 - Bound ruin

No catastrophic tail risk appears. If the message were a true emergency, the analysis would change.

Action from TRC: no tail-risk rejection.

## Step 4 - Preserve structure

The family dinner structure is a recurring trust-building pattern. Repeated interruption can degrade the structure even if one interruption seems small.

Action from CSV: preserve the dinner structure unless an actual emergency exists.

## Step 5 - Score the ripples lightly

| Option                                             | Immediate work ripple | Family ripple | Long-run pattern                                 | Tier 1 result         |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------: | ------------: | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------- |
| Reply now at table                                 | Small positive        | Negative      | Normalizes interruption                          | Not preferred         |
| Say “I will reply after dinner” and put phone away | Neutral/slight delay  | Positive      | Strengthens presence and trust                   | Preferred             |
| Leave table for five minutes to reply              | Small positive        | Mild negative | Better than table-scrolling but still interrupts | Backup only if needed |

## Tier 1 conclusion

Best ordinary action: put the phone away, finish dinner, and reply afterward.

Five-sentence public rationale: The message was not an emergency. The family dinner structure mattered because repeated attention fractures create cumulative harm. No rights or tail-risk condition required immediate work response. The best option preserved presence while still allowing the parent to answer later. The decision can update if new information shows the message was urgent.

## What this teaches

MathGov is not “score everything.” The rhythm is: ground reality, protect what must not be traded away, check ruin, preserve viable structures, then rank the remaining options.
