## Capability Claim Integrity implementation

A material capability claim now requires the following record before it may acquire gate, authority, deployment, conformance, or public-communication weight:

| Required field | Function |
|---|---|
| Operational definition | States what the capability means in measurable functional terms for the pinned configuration and domain. |
| Observable criteria | Defines the observations that would support or fail the claim. |
| Mechanism or system composition | Identifies the components and interactions that instantiate the claimed capability. |
| Evidence surface | Identifies traces, trials, records, or measurements supporting the claim. |
| Validity conditions | Bounds the configurations, domains, contexts, and controls under which the claim holds. |
| Alternative explanations | Records scripting, operator intervention, memorization, benchmark fit, proxy behavior, or other material alternatives. |
| Falsification or revision trigger | States what evidence forces narrowing, reopening, or refusal. |
| Evidence maturity stage | Prevents implementation evidence from being promoted into physical, operational, or outcome validation. |
| Permitted claim | States the strongest wording the evidence supports. |
| Explicit nonclaims | Prevents silent inference to consciousness, personhood, moral agency, generality, safety, legality, legitimacy, or authority. |

This rule avoids two opposing errors. It rejects anthropomorphic or marketing overclaim, but it also rejects composition reductionism. A configured system may possess a bounded operational capability through coordinated inference, memory, planning, feedback, tools, deterministic constraints, and human controls even though no single component possesses the whole capability. That does not make the system conscious, morally authoritative, generally autonomous, or safe beyond the evidence boundary.
