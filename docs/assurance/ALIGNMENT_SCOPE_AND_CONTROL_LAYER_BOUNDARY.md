# Alignment Scope and Control-Layer Boundary

**Status:** Informative boundary companion for MathGov Core v12.6.

## Current grounded claim

MathGov is the umbrella ethical governance framework. RippleLogic is the rights-constrained, ruin-aware, structurally qualified decision architecture inside MathGov. It can govern candidate actions, policies, deployments, system changes, institutional choices, and agent behavior across a declared decision surface.

MathGov is not, by itself, a training algorithm, an inner-alignment proof, a mechanistic-interpretability method, a cybersecurity control stack, a formal-verification engine, a legal authority, or a domain safety certification system.

## Long-horizon aspiration and present evidence boundary

The long-horizon design target is a broadly universalizable, union-grounded ethical operating architecture for intelligence-aligned decision-making in shared reality. That is an aspiration and research direction. The current v12.6 claim is narrower: a Tier 1-3 public research/source specification, assurance scaffold, and pilot-preparation architecture.

## Descriptive-to-normative bridge

MathGov does not derive an entire ethic from physics or interconnection alone.

1. **Operational-descriptive premise:** decisions occur in layered and intersecting structures of causal, informational, social, biological, ecological, and institutional interdependence.
2. **Explicit normative entry:** MathGov adopts rights protection, non-domination, welfare concern, precaution under serious uncertainty, remedy, and accountable participation as governance commitments.
3. **Operational architecture:** RippleLogic orders those commitments through Reality Grounding, RF/NCRC, TRC, CSV, and residual RLS.

"We are one" may express interdependence, solidarity, or a metaphysical horizon. Only the operational interdependence claim is part of standard Tier 1-3 computation. The metaphysical horizon cannot override evidence, rights, ruin protection, structural viability, lawful authority, or refusal.

## Full-stack control map

| Control layer | MathGov relationship | Current boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Objective and specification design | Can structure rights, risk, stakeholder, and welfare requirements | Does not prove that a learned system internalizes the intended objective |
| Training-time behavioral alignment | Can govern objectives, data practices, oversight, and acceptance criteria | Does not replace preference learning, RLHF/RLAIF, constitutional training, or other training methods |
| Inner alignment and model internals | Can require evidence and narrow claims | Does not prove absence of mesa-optimization, deceptive alignment, or goal misgeneralization |
| Evaluations and red teaming | Can define acceptance, escalation, and refusal records | Requires external test suites and empirical evidence |
| Agentic and cybersecurity controls | Agent System specifies controls and threat mappings | Requires executable security tests, telemetry, local architecture, and incident response |
| Socio-technical governance | Primary current strength | Still requires lawful mandate, participation, appeal, domain expertise, and independent accountability |
| Physical or causal execution | Can require PC-AEP evidence and refuse unsupported action | Does not create engineering, medical, ecological, or formal safety proof |

## Claim wording

Supportable: "MathGov provides a governance-layer decision and assurance architecture for intelligence-aligned, rights-protecting, ripple-aware choices."

Not supportable in this release: "MathGov has solved AI alignment," "MathGov is scientifically proven as the best universal OS," or "a passing MathGov run proves that an AI model is internally aligned or safe to deploy."
